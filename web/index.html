            Manage in StartOS

            Buy, Renew and Reset Bandwidth can be started right from this dashboard or from Services → TunnelSats → Actions. Every payment raises a Pay Invoice task on your node and shows the same invoice here.

            • Buy Subscription A new tunnel on the configured node: pick a server region and a plan.
            • Renew Subscription Extends the current subscription. Your node and its configuration stay as they are.
            • Reset Bandwidth From 70% of this month's allowance: resets the counter for a small fee (exact sats on the invoice), a limited number of times per month.
            • Connect Wallet Enable or disable automatic NWC (NIP-47) renewals, view the 1.2× recommended budget and optionally route relay traffic via Tor.
            • Import Subscription Use an existing TunnelSats WireGuard configuration.
            • Configure Switch TunnelSats off, change the target node or replace the configuration.
            • Export WireGuard Configuration Shows the full configuration, including its private key.

            Check your privacy on the node

            Kill switch: The tunnel and its routing belong to your Lightning node package, not to TunnelSats. With LND 0.21.3-beta:10, Core Lightning 26.6.8:3 or Eclair 0.14.3:3 or later (required by this package), clearnet traffic is dropped instead of leaving through your home connection if wg0 goes down or is removed. Tor keeps working. Turning the tunnel off on purpose returns clearnet traffic to your home connection.

            This dashboard runs in the TunnelSats container and cannot see inside your node, so it never reports the tunnel as verified. Open a shell on the node with start-cli package attach lnd and run:

            • wg show wg0 a recent handshake and non-zero transfer
            • ip rule a rule with lookup 51820
            • ip route show table 51820 default dev wg0
            • curl -4 -s https://ifconfig.me must print the TunnelSats server IPv4, not your home IP
            • curl -6 -s https://ifconfig.me must print the TunnelSats server IPv6 (or fail when IPv6 is blocked), never your home IPv6

            These checks show the tunnel while it is up; they do not test what happens when it goes down.

            Frequently asked questions

            How does TunnelSats hide my home IP?

            The WireGuard tunnel runs inside your Lightning node's own container (LND, Core Lightning or Eclair), brought up by the node's clearnet VPN action:

            • Inbound: peers connect to the TunnelSats server address and forwarded port; the traffic arrives at your node through wg0.
            • Outbound: policy routing (table 51820) sends the node's clearnet traffic (handshakes, gossip, ping/pong) through the tunnel while it is up.
            • Kill switch: the tunnel and its routing belong to your Lightning node package. With the required node versions, clearnet traffic is dropped if wg0 goes down instead of falling back to your home connection.
            • Tor keeps working: onion connections continue over the container network as before.

            Nothing is configured box-wide: no StartOS gateways, no interface firewall changes. Only the target node uses the tunnel.

            How do I buy a subscription?
            1. Run Services → TunnelSats → Actions → Buy Subscription and pick your node (LND, Core Lightning or Eclair), a server region and a plan.
            2. The action generates the WireGuard keys on your server; the private key never leaves it.
            3. Accept the Pay Invoice task on your node, or pay the invoice from the action result with any wallet.
            4. Once paid, TunnelSats saves the configuration and asks your node to route through the tunnel. Accept that task on the node.

            This dashboard does not take payments: every purchase runs through the action, is paid with your node's Pay Invoice task and is finished by TunnelSats itself.

            How do renewals work? What happens when it expires?
            • Run the Renew Subscription action at any time; the invoice is paid through a Pay Invoice task on your node. The WireGuard key and your node's configuration stay as they are.
            • TunnelSats raises a Renew Subscription task 7 days before expiry, updates it at 3 days and again once it has expired, and posts StartOS notifications at the same points.
            • If it expires: TunnelSats disables the tunnel on its server, so your node's clearnet peer connections through TunnelSats stop until you renew or turn off the clearnet VPN on your node.
            Can renewals happen automatically?

            Yes, via Nostr Wallet Connect (NWC). Run Services → TunnelSats → Actions → Connect Wallet in StartOS and paste a nostr+walletconnect:// URI from Alby Hub, LNbits or Clams. Set a spending budget at least 1.2× your expected renewal price (in sats) on your wallet to absorb BTC/USD rate swings, and optionally route relay traffic through Tor. If auto-renewal ever fails or exceeds your budget, TunnelSats automatically falls back to raising a manual Pay Invoice task on your node.

            How does the 100 GB monthly allowance work?

            Each subscription includes 100 GB of transfer per calendar month, reset on the 1st (00:00 UTC). Above 100 GB, TunnelSats restricts the VPN connection. From 70% usage, the Reset Bandwidth action resets the counter for a small fee; resets per month are limited.

            How is IPv6 handled?

            Your Lightning node package decides how IPv6 is routed. Current builds send IPv6 through the tunnel when the configuration's AllowedIPs include ::/0 (TunnelSats configurations do), and block it otherwise.

            The Allow IPv6 Endpoint setting only lets TunnelSats hand your node an IPv6 server endpoint to announce.

            How do I back up my configuration?

            Run Export WireGuard Configuration in StartOS to copy the full configuration. It is also part of StartOS backups. The dashboard never shows the private key.
